# Reserve risk review — Australian PL and PI, December 2022
**Net of reinsurance | A$ million | accident years 2012–2022 | APRA General Insurance Claims Development Statistics, December 2022**

**Reserve adequacy.** On the repaired net paid chain ladder, AY2012–2022 outstanding claims are A$4,493.75m (PL A$3,189.03m, PI A$1,304.72m) against booked outstanding of A$8,706m — booked exceeds model by A$4,212.25m. Read that as a scope difference, not headroom: our model stops at development year 10 and excludes accident years before 2012, while APRA's booked ultimate carries the tail. It is not evidence for release.

The more informative signal is PI modelled IBNR of **negative A$380.28m**. The paid pattern projects ultimates below reported case estimates, and modelled outstanding is itself negative for AY2013–2017. We have retained those negatives rather than flooring them. Either PI case estimates are redundant or the paid pattern is inappropriate — PI link ratios fall below 1.000 from development age 6 because published cumulative net payments decline, reflecting recoveries and revisions. Resolve this before drawing any PI conclusion.

**Prior-year development.** The 2022 runoff for AY2012–2021 is favourable. Payments of A$913m ran A$574.19m below the opening expectation of A$1,487.19m, giving a model claims development result of **+A$1,383.76m** (PL +A$501.96m, PI +A$881.80m). Booked development was nearly flat at **+A$49m** (PL −A$217m, PI +A$266m). The gap is the point: industry booked ultimates barely moved while our paid projection released heavily, which suggests the model is reacting to slow payment experience that reserving actuaries did not accept as genuine improvement. AY2022 is excluded and enters closing reserves separately. The opening position is reconstructed from the December 2022 publication, not the archived December 2021 vintage.

**Relative uncertainty.** PI is materially more uncertain. Ultimate prediction standard errors are A$201.37m for PL (6.3% of outstanding) and A$344.51m for PI (26.4%) — PI carries the larger absolute error on a reserve a quarter the size, with less stable development factors and reserves concentrated in recent accident years. These are ultimate runoff errors over the full remaining development, not one-year figures; the runoff result above is a separate measure and the two must not be added or compared.

**Diversification.** Place limited reliance on it. Portfolio standard error rises from A$399.05m at zero correlation to A$478.13m at 0.5 and A$545.88m at perfect dependence, so the benefit falls from A$146.84m to A$67.75m to nil. Those correlations are assumptions, not estimates: inter-class dependence cannot be measured from APRA aggregates. PL and PI share legal, inflationary and judicial drivers, so independence is implausible; we would plan on no more than the 0.5 scenario.

**Principal risks.** PI case-estimate adequacy and the negative IBNR; tail exposure beyond development 10, which is unmodelled, so every standard error here is understated; common calendar-year and superimposed-inflation effects that breach Mack's independence assumptions; and the use of industry aggregates as a benchmark for any individual portfolio. The two-standard-error figures are sensitivities, not percentiles or capital requirements.
